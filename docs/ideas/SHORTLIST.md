## My suggested first 5 experiments

1. **Cash Drawer Count** — simple fast daily job, owner-operated business, offline, repeatable. Counterfactual: POS solutions may already do this adequately. Interview five shop owners first.
2. **Invoice Number Ledger** — friction around numbering and reconciliation. Counterfactual: invoicing tools already solve it; find an offline/ultra-simple niche.
3. **Freelance Deposit Tracker** — money-linked need with a short decision chain. Counterfactual: generic accounting software may win on integration.
4. **Moving Box Inventory** — unusually clear search intent and app UX; likely event-driven rather than sticky retention. Favor lifetime pricing and avoid subscription.
5. **Reptile Feeding Journal** — narrow, repeated niche; validate that hobbyists pay rather than using notes/spreadsheets.

This shortlist deliberately mixes business tasks, event-driven utilities and specialist hobbies. It is a **test queue**, not a launch queue. The top-ranked scoring table and this diversified starting five may differ.

## Most important challenge

**100 ideas is not 100 opportunities.** Writing code is now cheaper than distribution. For every idea:
- Find 10–20 competitor listings and real App Store query suggestions in one target locale.
- Record first-page saturation, review complaints, functional gaps and observable user intent.
- Interview 5–10 relevant users, then ask for one hard commitment: paid preorder, payment-intent signup, data import, or switching an existing workflow.
- Test one differentiating screen/flow before building the whole app.
- Do not greenlight a batch of nearly identical products.

## Decision rules (proposed gates, not industry benchmarks)

- **Go to MVP:** at least 3 of 10 qualified users demonstrate the problem or existing workaround; one clear differentiation thesis; credible ASO distribution path; no unmanageable policy/privacy burden.
- **Consider paid pilot:** at least 2 independent people agree to pay or provide equivalent costly effort after a real workflow demo.
- **Stop or pivot:** cannot find qualified users, no meaningful competing-product weakness, or proposed product is substantially indistinguishable under App Store guideline 4.3.
- **Cap work:** 1–2 concurrent experiments; spend at most one week on discovery and one MVP before first observed market feedback unless field evidence justifies more.

## Where the estimates can mislead

- Strong search intent does not guarantee weak competition or affordable organic acquisition.
- High repeat use does not guarantee willingness to pay.
- Standalone apps can create App Store spam/review risk if they replicate existing tools.
- Sensitive logs (children, medications, financial details) need stronger disclosure, security and recovery than the first development sketch.
