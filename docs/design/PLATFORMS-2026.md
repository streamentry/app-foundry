# Platform design in 2026: trends without gimmicks

_Research checked: 2026-11-14. Re-evaluate whenever the platform HIG changes._

## iOS: Liquid Glass is not an all-purpose effect

Apple's HIG describes Liquid Glass as a functional layer for controls and navigation, **separate from the branded content layer**. The recommendation is to preserve familiar system chrome and put branded illustration, imagery and typography in the content. Avoid blurred content cards that lower contrast. For edge-to-edge layouts, verify safe areas, scroll chrome, keyboard and reduction settings. See [Apple: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials), [Apple: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), and [WWDC26: Communicate your brand identity](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/251/).

## Android: Material behavior without a Material-looking clone

Use semantic colors, accessibility patterns, navigation and adaptive size classes. Allow platform-specific controls rather than insisting on pixel-identical UIs. Brand difference belongs to information hierarchy, editorial choices and content visualization. See [Android: Material 3](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/designsystems/material3), [Android: Mobile UI](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile), and [Android: Theming](https://developer.android.com/codelabs/m3-design-theming).

## 2026 aesthetic hypotheses

Quiet editorial typography, deliberate motion, warm tactility, purpose-driven spatial depth, strong numerals and restrained illustration are useful **stylistic options**. They are not causal evidence of conversion. Never impose a style because a trend article lists it.

## Decision test

For any visual device, ask: does it improve task recognition, reading speed, touch accuracy, trust, learnability or emotional appropriateness? If not, remove it. Validate with target users, especially older adults and caregivers.
