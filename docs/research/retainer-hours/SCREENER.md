# Retainer-hours study — qualification and consent

Recruit 10–15 potential participants to find five qualified and independent freelancers. This research is for people who personally track and reconcile **prepaid client hours**, not simply anyone who bills by the hour.

## Ask these four questions BEFORE showing the app

1. Have you had a customer pay for a fixed number of work hours in the last six months (not just fixed project price or time-and-materials)?
2. Walk me through the last time you checked remaining hours: what tool, who did it, and what happened?
3. How often does this reconciliation really occur? What goes wrong if the count is late or inaccurate?
4. Who chooses the tools and who verifies the client's work-hour summary? Could the client refuse a separate app?

Qualify only people personally responsible for a real prepaid-hours allowance, with a describable existing workflow. Keep unrelated fixed-price users out of the five task slots. Recruitment rejection counts can be recorded separately; don't invent replacement observations.

## Consent

"We are testing a prototype, not you. Could I watch you complete two fictional client scenarios over 15–20 minutes and record anonymous timings, error counts and your views? You can withdraw at any point. We don't need real client details, passwords, screen recordings or contact information in our research repo. Is that okay?"

Record only after an affirmative answer. If the user declines or asks to stop, stop and do not retain their data.

## Recruitment

Start with warm founder-led contacts or trusted introductions. Community posts may be used only where the moderation rules allow research recruitment. No mass unsolicited DMs, no fabricated participants, and no private contact details committed to this repo. If you cannot recruit five genuine eligible users, that is itself a negative distribution signal.

Allocate P01–P05 only after qualification. Avoid leading with claims about saved time or expected outcomes.
